I chose 3 to run: nucleotide diversity (pi), that calculates the average proportion of genetic differences between any two seqs picked at random from a group, segregating sites (S), which is the total count of alignment positions (columns) where at least one sample has a different DNA base, and haplotype diversity (Hd), which is the probability that two randomly chosen individuals have different unique seq variations/haplotypes. Since the acient DNA is damaged or incomplete the script will: 1) drop any alignment column that contains missing data (N's or gaps) in *any* of the 42 samples, leaving 11,153 clean positions, 2) measure both pre-bottleneck and post-bottleneck across the exact same sequence positions (if trimmed separately it would be comparing different regions of the genome which could skew the results), and 3) will re-run nucleotide diversity (pi) using *pairwise deletion* which skips missing data pair by pair.

11. Hypothesis testing: Is this gap between the two numbers bigger than chance would produce? 
**H0: Pre and Post have the same nucleotide diversity, so the group labels carry no information.**
**H1: Post pi is lower than Pre pi.**
The test is one-sided because the founder-effect hypothesis predicts a direction. 
*Why a permutation test?* The 42 samples ARE NOT independent random draws from a bell curve. They are related to each other and share ancestry, so a t-test assumptions won't work. A *permutation* test avoids those assumptions with the logic "*if the labels didn't matter, you could shuffle "Post" and "Pre" among the 42 sequnces, keep the groups sizes at 14 and 28, and get a difference in pi about as large as the real one*". The script reshuffles 10,000 times and counts how often chance matche or beats the actual numbers I observed in `diversity_stats.R`. 
**3 analyses run:** (1) Permutation test on all 42 samples (main test), (2) Same test without the GilbertM20, GilbertM21, and Oimyakon (checks that the result does not depend on tha ancient divergent clade which inflates the Pre pi number), (3) Compares Post against 10,000 random draws of 14 samples (fixes the 14-vs-28 mismatch which matters for the S and Hd counts). The script computes the full 42x42 distance matrix once and takes averages of pieces of it. 
**Limitations:** (1) One locus. The mitochondrial genome is a single non-recombining unit, so it carries one gene geneaology.
